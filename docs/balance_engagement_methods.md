# High-Balance, Salary, Engagement and Tenure Methods (Phase 5)

Implemented in `src/churn_analytics/balance_engagement.py`, reported by `balance_engagement_report.py`, drawn by
`visualization.py`, and covered by `tests/test_balance_engagement*.py`. Results: `reports/high_balance_engagement_analysis.md`
(generated) and `reports/figures/*.png` (generated by `scripts/make_figures.py`). Settings: `[balance_engagement]` in the config.

## What is analysed

* **High-balance customers:** who they are, churn inside the group, and whether they churn differently from comparable
  Low-balance customers.
* **Balance level and salary:** churn across equal-sized groups of non-zero balances and of salary; salary x balance grids.
* **Engagement and tenure:** the inactive-minus-active gap inside every segment, who is inactive, engagement by tenure
  group and by number of products, and churn for each tenure year.
* **Balance exposure proxy:** where the balance held by churned customers sits, and how concentrated it is.

"High-balance" means a balance above the median of non-zero balances. Neither balance nor salary is profitability, revenue or
lifetime value. All results are descriptive associations in one snapshot; no model is fitted and no table controls for
every variable.

## Measures

| Measure | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| High-minus-Low gap | churn rate of High-balance minus Low-balance customers, **non-zero balances only**, in percentage points | Newcombe 95% interval. Zero-balance customers are a different group (and one country has none), so they are left out |
| Directly standardised gap | within each stratum (e.g. each country) take the High and Low rates; average both with the **same weights** (each stratum's share of non-zero customers); gap = High average minus Low average | Removes differences in customer mix between the groups for the chosen strata. Strata where either group has fewer than `min_reportable_group_size` customers are left out and the weights re-scaled; `coverage` = share of non-zero customers represented |
| Standardised interval | `gap +/- z x sqrt(sum w^2 [p_H(1-p_H)/n_H + p_L(1-p_L)/n_L])` | Normal approximation; labelled approximate in the report |
| Equal-sized balance / salary groups | cut points are quantiles computed from the data (right-closed); number of groups in the config | Cut points and ranges are printed in the report. Not added to the processed data |
| Spearman rank correlation | between salary and balance (non-zero balances; and all customers) | -1 to +1; undefined if either column has no variation |
| Engagement gap | churn rate of inactive minus active members inside a segment, pp, Newcombe interval | Same definition as the Engagement Drop Indicator |
| Share inactive | inactive customers / customers in the segment, Wilson interval | Who is disengaged |
| Balance exposure proxy | sum of Balance over churned customers (per segment) | **Not lost revenue.** Also shown as share of all churned balance, share of all balance, and share of the segment's own balance |
| Concentration | share of churned balance held by the top 10% / 20% / 50% of churned customers (largest balances first) | Churned customers with a zero balance add nothing; their number is reported |

## Rules applied

* Empty groups are n/a (never 0); below 30 customers a rate is withheld; below 100 it is flagged *small sample*.
* No customer is lost: every table's groups add back up to all customers (tested).
* Sentences describing a trend, a relationship or a verdict ("no steady rise", "no detectable trend", "do differ") are
  **computed from the numbers** (monotonic check, rank correlation and p-value, interval excludes zero), not fixed text. Facts about
  the data, such as which country has no zero-balance customers, are also computed (tested by changing the inputs).
* The largest engagement gap is reported among well-populated segments; a larger gap in a small sample is mentioned only as a caveat.

## Limitations

* Stratification and standardisation reduce differences in country (and age) mix only; they do not adjust for every other variable.
* The data cannot explain why segments differ, why a country has no zero-balance customers, or why extreme rates occur in small groups.
* Many segments are compared, so some differences will look larger by chance, especially in small groups.
* Inactivity may be a symptom of leaving; the engagement gap does not show which way any influence runs.
* One snapshot, three countries; the meaning of `Exited` is an assumption (D6); provenance is unconfirmed.
